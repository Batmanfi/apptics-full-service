What Apptics connects

One system for what happens after the customer decides to buy.

01

What exactly is Apptics?

Apptics is a checkout-to-chargeback revenue system for established ecommerce brands. It combines customizable checkout flows, subscription infrastructure, supported payment processing options, and chargeback protection.

The team audits the current setup, recommends the components that make economic sense, integrates them with the store, and supports ongoing optimization. It is not simply another checkout app, payment processor, or chargeback dashboard.

02

Is Apptics suitable for a brand like ours?

Apptics is primarily built for established ecommerce and retail brands, generally generating more than $50,000 per month, that already know how to acquire customers and want to improve what happens after the click.

The strongest fit is usually a brand with a measurable opportunity in at least one of these areas: checkout value, subscription conversion, recurring-payment continuity, processor resilience, or chargeback reduction. If there is not enough volume or a meaningful economic problem to solve, changing the infrastructure may not be worthwhile.

03

Is Apptics a payment processor?

Apptics is not presented as a single standalone processor. Apptics Pay helps eligible brands access supported payment processors and merchant accounts, including options for both lower-risk and higher-risk businesses.

Where approved and technically supported, transactions or failed payments may use additional payment routes. The available routes, rates, approval requirements, and underwriting timeline depend on the business, product, entity, geography, volume, and risk profile.

04

Do we need Shopify Plus or a completely new stack?

No. Apptics checkout flows do not automatically require Shopify Plus, and an implementation does not automatically mean replacing every tool in the current stack.

The Revenue Audit identifies what should remain, what should change, and whether the rollout should happen in stages. A stable component should not be replaced simply to make the stack look more uniform.

05

What happens to our existing subscribers?

Apptics can tokenize and vault eligible subscriber payment credentials so the recurring-revenue asset is less dependent on a single processor. Supported configurations may allow eligible rebills to move across approved gateways.

However, existing subscribers are not automatically portable. Token compatibility, processor rules, consent requirements, gateway support, and the current subscription setup must be reviewed before any migration. The implementation plan should define what can move, how it will be tested, and how the team will roll back if the new configuration does not behave as expected.

06

How long does setup take?

There is no honest universal timeline. It depends on the components selected, the current stack, data and access availability, integration complexity, processor underwriting, and whether subscriber migration is involved.

During the Revenue Audit, the team will map the likely timeline, dependencies, and required people for the prospect’s specific setup.

07

What does Apptics cost?

Pricing depends on the products used and the business’s processing profile.

Based on the current offer structure, Apptics Checkout is priced at $999 per month plus 2% and $0.30 per transaction. Payment-processing fees are generally 1.8%–3% for lower-risk accounts and 3%–5% for higher-risk accounts, subject to underwriting and the approved processing arrangement. Apptics Shield pricing depends on the dispute program and is intended to improve on the brand’s current alert economics where possible.

The Revenue Audit should compare the complete cost against the measurable upside. A platform that raises gross revenue but produces worse contribution margin is not a win.

08

How does the 90 day MRR guarantee work?

The guarantee is not a blanket promise that applies to every store. Eligibility is established during the audit.

Before implementation, Apptics and the client document the starting MRR baseline, the 90-day measurement period, required actions, attribution method, exclusions, and the platform fees covered by the guarantee. If the agreed MRR target is not reached and the documented conditions have been met, the applicable platform fees are returned according to those written terms.

Ask to see the complete guarantee terms before agreeing to the rollout.

09

What is the genuine worst case?

The real risk is disrupting a stable revenue system to solve a problem that was not large enough—or compatible enough—to justify the change.

An implementation can disappoint when the product lacks a legitimate subscription use case, token or processor compatibility is limited, underwriting does not produce the required approval, the migration is rushed, or checkout changes lift gross AOV while hurting margin, refunds, or customer experience.

That is why the first step is an audit rather than an automatic migration. A responsible recommendation may be to use only one Apptics component, stage the rollout, or leave a part of the current system unchanged.
