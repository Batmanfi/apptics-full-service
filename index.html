You’re acquiring customers. Keeping the revenue is the harder part.

An order converts, but the job isn't done. That customer still has to buy the right offer, get billed successfully, and stay out of the dispute queue - and a weak link anywhere in that path quietly erodes what looked like a good acquisition.

Every month, you’re paying to replace one-time buyers

  • Customers buy products they’d happily buy again - checkout still ends the relationship at one order
  • The next sale depends on them remembering you, not on your system bringing them back
  • An upsell doesn’t fix whether the subscription converts or the next payment even goes through

Recurring revenue depends on a processor staying available

  • A subscriber in a dashboard is not the same as a rebill that actually goes through.
  • If their payment details sit with one processor, a restriction or shutdown can freeze that revenue.
  • The subscriptions may still exist. The path to collect them may not.

You have to connect checkout, payments, and disputes

  • Checkout, subscriptions, payments, and chargebacks live in different tools.
  • When collections drop or disputes rise, your team has to figure out which vendor owns the problem.
  • You see pieces of the money. You don’t see the full path from “buy” to kept revenue.

Here's what most people do:

Sending more traffic into the same checkout

More acquisition can grow sales, but it does not improve the value of an order, recover a failed rebill, or make subscriber credentials more portable. If those are the constraints, more volume increases the amount moving through the same weak points.

Adding another app and leaving the connections to your team

A specialist tool can be useful. But installing it does not decide how the checkout offer, subscription terms, billing, payment routing, and dispute handling should work together—or who will keep them aligned after launch.

Fixing payment problems only when they become urgent

A processor issue or dispute spike gives you a reason to act, but fewer comfortable options for doing it. Backup processing and credential portability are easier to evaluate while the existing setup is still functioning.

You don’t just need more tools; you need checkout, recurring billing, payments, and dispute protection built to work together, with one team coordinating the implementation.

Questions worth answering before you change anything

1. What will the full-service engagement cost?

Your total depends on the scope, transaction volume, processing setup, and support required. Apptics currently lists Enterprise at $2,499 per month plus transaction fees, but that is not a complete quote for every component described here. Your proposal needs to separate platform, processing, alert, implementation, and other applicable charges so you can evaluate the all-in cost. Current plans.

2. What makes Apptics worth trusting with checkout and billing?

The public team and customer evidence give you a starting point, not a reason to skip diligence. Before committing, review the relevant implementation scope, supported integrations, security documentation, contractual responsibilities, and comparable customer evidence. Your decision should rest on whether the actual setup fits your business, not just the size of a testimonial.

3. Could changing systems disrupt existing orders or subscriptions?

Changing revenue infrastructure carries risk, which is why migration details need to be resolved before live changes. Your plan should address compatibility, testing, launch responsibilities, and what happens if a change needs to be reversed. We start with the current setup and proposed scope; this offer does not promise a zero-risk migration or automatic compatibility with every tool.

4. What if MRR doesn’t double within 90 days?

For eligible brands, the offer provides a refund of platform fees covered by the written guarantee. Your agreement determines the starting baseline, measurement window, required actions, exclusions, and refund process. It does not automatically cover every cost or revenue loss associated with the business.

5. Do I need to replace Shopify, Stripe, and every existing app?

The scope depends on what you use today and which parts need to change. Apptics offers integrations with existing tools, but your specific combination must be checked rather than assumed compatible. The audit is where you distinguish a useful replacement from a system that should stay in place.

6. Can you move my subscribers if a processor shuts down—or if I leave Apptics?

Vaulting is intended to improve portability across supported processors, not make every credential universally transferable. The answer depends on the token, merchant identity, receiving processor, and applicable technical and compliance requirements. Before signing, establish what can be exported, which destinations are supported, and what migration assistance and charges apply to your account.

7. Does Shield prevent chargebacks by refunding the order?

Some pre-dispute alerts are resolved through refunds before a formal chargeback develops. That can help protect your ratio, but a refunded order is not revenue retained in full. Evaluate alert fees, refunds, chargeback costs, and the broader processing effect together when deciding whether the configuration makes financial sense.

8. How much work will my team have to do?

You provide access, product and subscription decisions, the relevant business numbers, and approvals needed for the agreed implementation. Apptics handles the scoped onboarding, setup, integrations, and continuing support. Done-for-you should reduce configuration work on your side, not remove you from decisions about what customers buy or how your business operates.

9. How long does implementation take?

The schedule depends on your existing tools, migration requirements, processor approvals, and the selected components. A straightforward setup and an established subscription migration should not be given the same timetable without review. Confirm your launch milestones separately from the 90-day MRR measurement window rather than assuming the guarantee clock starts on the booking date.

10. Can you help if I don’t offer subscriptions or operate in a higher-risk category?

Checkout, payment, and dispute needs can exist without a subscription offer, but the MRR promise requires a suitable recurring-revenue opportunity and eligibility. Apptics Pay markets processing support for higher-risk ecommerce categories; acceptance still depends on the business, geography, and underwriting. The audit should determine which parts of the offer fit, rather than forcing the whole package onto every store. Payment services.

11. What support continues after setup?

The supplied full-service offer includes biweekly support and optimization calls plus a dedicated service manager with a 12-hour response target. That is different from a guarantee that any incident will be fixed in twelve hours. Confirm the support scope, escalation route, and responsibilities in your agreement so expectations are clear before launch.

12. What happens when I request the audit?

You choose a time to discuss your store and the areas you want improved. We review your current checkout, subscriptions, payment setup, and disputes to identify where Apptics may fit. You can then discuss scope, pricing, compatibility, and the guarantee before deciding whether to proceed; the immediate next step is a business review, not a migration.
